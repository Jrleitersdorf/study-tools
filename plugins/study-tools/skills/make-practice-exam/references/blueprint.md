# The blueprint: what the practice exam will contain

The blueprint turns the confirmed exam profile (`exam-format.md`) into an exact list of question slots. Teachers call it a table of specifications: content down one side, question type and difficulty across the other, counts in the cells. It is what makes a practice exam feel like the real one rather than a pile of questions on the right topics.

Write it to `work/blueprint.md`.

## 1. Inputs

- The confirmed profile in `course/exam-format.md`: sections, types, counts, points, credit rules, time, difficulty distribution, coverage, style.
- The scope for this exam (`course/course.md`): which units.
- The student's choice of emphasis: **like the real exam** (default) or **weighted toward my weak spots** (`course/weak-spots.md`). With weak-spot weighting, move about a quarter of the slots toward weak units and say so on the exam page.

## 2. Slots

One row per question slot, in the real exam's order:

| # | Section | Type (page type) | Points | Unit or topic | Demand | Steps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | multiple choice (mc) | 2 | Unit 3 | apply | 2 | distractor: the sign error |
| 14 | B | short answer (written) | 6 | Units 2 and 4 | analyze | 3 | compare the two methods |

Rules:
- **Counts and points** match the profile exactly when the practice exam covers the whole real exam. When it covers only part of the scope, scale them by that share and say so in the instructions.
- **Coverage** follows the profile's shares; every unit in scope gets at least one slot.
- **Difficulty** follows the profile's distribution: the same share of recall, application and multi-step items, and the same kind of hardest item. Real exams are usually a little harder than the practice material students see; when the evidence is homework or a study guide rather than a past exam, lean one notch harder.
- **Style traits** carry over: if the real exam uses data tables in stems, so does this one; if it asks for a one-line justification, so does this one.
- **Time**: the practice exam's time is the real one's (scaled by share). Check the slots fit: add the minutes each slot needs to solve by hand; if the sum exceeds the time, the slots are too hard or too many.

## 3. Rounds

A student who wants to practice more than once gets separate rounds (Round 1, Round 2), each a full exam built from the same blueprint, with no question repeated across rounds and the topics within each unit varied. Default: one round; offer a second at delivery.

## 4. Show it with the profile

The student confirms the profile and the blueprint together, in one compact message: the profile's section table, then the blueprint summarized as counts per unit and type, the time, and the emphasis. Do not show all slots unless asked.
