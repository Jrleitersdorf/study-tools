# Exam profile: ECON 101 Midterm 1 (made-up example)

Evidence: `exams/midterm1-fall.pdf` (last year's Midterm 1, taken by the student, open mode); the syllabus's exam paragraph; the instructor's review-session remarks (lecture 9 transcript, minute 41). Confirmed by the student.

## Logistics

50 minutes, 30 points, 12 questions in three sections. Closed book; a basic calculator is allowed; no formula sheet. On paper in class.

## Sections

| Section | Type | Count, points | Answer form | Credit rules | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | multiple choice (mc), 4 options | 8 × 2 | a letter | no penalty for wrong answers | about 2 min each |
| B | short calculation (num) | 2 × 3 | a number with units | partial credit for correct setup (on paper only) | about 5 min each |
| C | short answer (written) | 2 × 4 | 4 to 6 sentences, a sketch of a graph allowed | rubric points; "explain the mechanism, not just the direction" | about 9 min each |

## Difficulty

About 35% recall of definitions, 45% one-step applications to a new scenario, 20% multi-step problems (equilibrium, then a surplus or an elasticity). Distractors are real mistakes: a shift of the wrong curve, the direction of a change reversed, absolute advantage taken for comparative advantage, the legal payer of a tax taken for who bears it.

Anchors: easy, a definition stated in plain words with three plausible misdefinitions; middle, a news-style scenario that asks which curve shifts and which way; hard, two linear curves, solve for equilibrium, then compute a surplus.

## Coverage

Units 1 to 4: supply, demand and surplus (about 30%), elasticity (25%), price controls and taxes (35%), trade and comparative advantage (10%). Unit 5 (externalities) is not on Midterm 1.

## Style

Scenarios from news stories (a drought, a new tax). Prices in dollars with round numbers. "Which of the following" stems; no "all of the above". Section C asks "explain why", and the instructor marks down answers that give only a direction.

## Instructor signals

"Know how to compute consumer surplus from linear curves, and know the midpoint formula" (lecture 9, minute 41). "Tax incidence will be on it" (same).

## Unknowns

- Whether Section C answers need a drawn graph: assumed optional (the past exam's model answers mention one but award no marks for it).
