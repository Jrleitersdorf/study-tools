A sample's activity falls from 800 to 100 decays per second in 36 hours. What is its half-life?

  1. 8 hours
  2. 12 hours
  3. 18 hours
  4. 36 hours

800 to 100 is a factor of 8, three halvings, so \(t_{1/2}=36/3=12\) hours.

For any radioactive isotope, the activity of a sample halves over one half-life.

True: \(A=\lambda N\) and \(N\) halves.

An isotope has a half-life of 5 days. What fraction of a sample remains after 15 days?

Three half-lives: \((1/2)^3=1/8=0.125\).

Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5730 years. A wooden tool contains one quarter of the carbon-14 found in living wood. How old is it?

One quarter is two half-lives: \(2\times5730=11460\) years.

Radiocarbon dating assumes a sample's carbon-14 started at the level found in living tissue. Select every statement that is true.

  1. The clock starts when the organism stops taking in carbon.
  2. A sample with more carbon-14 than living tissue is very old.
  3. The age is \(t=\dfrac{t_{1/2}}{\ln 2}\ln\dfrac{N_0}{N}\).

(a) and (c) are the assumptions and the formula; (b) is impossible under the assumption, since \(N\le N_0\).

An isotope has decay constant \(\lambda=1\) per day. What is its half-life in days? An exact answer is fine.

\(t_{1/2}=\ln2/\lambda=\ln 2\approx0.693\) days.
