A radioactive sample has a half-life of 10 minutes. What fraction of the original nuclei is still undecayed after 30 minutes?

  1. 1/3
  2. 1/6
  3. 1/8
  4. 0, the sample is gone

30 minutes is three half-lives, so \((1/2)^3=1/8\) remains.

A radioactive isotope has decay constant \(\lambda\) and half-life \(t_{1/2}\). Select every statement that is true.

  1. \(t_{1/2}=\ln 2/\lambda\)
  2. An isotope with twice the decay constant has half the half-life.
  3. The activity of a sample does not depend on how many nuclei it contains.
  4. After two half-lives, one quarter of the nuclei remain.

(a) and (b) follow from \(t_{1/2}=\ln2/\lambda\); (d) is \((1/2)^2\). (c) is false because \(A=\lambda N\).

If one isotope has twice the decay constant of another, its half-life is also twice as long.

False. \(t_{1/2}=\ln 2/\lambda\), so doubling \(\lambda\) halves the half-life.

A radioactive sample has decay constant \(\lambda=0.5\) per hour. What fraction of the original nuclei remains undecayed after 2 hours? An exact answer such as e^(-1) is fine.

\(N/N_0=e^{-\lambda t}=e^{-0.5\cdot2}=e^{-1}\approx0.368\).
