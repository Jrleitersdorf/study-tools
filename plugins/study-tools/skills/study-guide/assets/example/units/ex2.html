Radiocarbon Dating

Covers the example slides 2 to 9 of lecture 2.

Takeaway

A living organism keeps its carbon-14 at a steady level; when it dies the carbon-14 decays with a half-life of 5730 years and is not replaced. Measuring the fraction \(N/N_0\) that is left and inverting the decay law gives the time since death, \(t=\frac{t_{1/2}}{\ln 2}\ln\frac{N_0}{N}\).

2.1Dating with a known half-life

Example slides 2 to 9

The decay law of 1.1 runs backwards as a clock: if you know how much a sample started with and how much is left, the elapsed time follows. For carbon-14 the starting level is the one in living tissue, which stays constant while the organism exchanges carbon with its surroundings.

Age from the fraction left
\[\begin{aligned}\frac{N}{N_0}&=e^{-\lambda t}\\ t&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{N_0}{N}=\frac{t_{1/2}}{\ln 2}\ln\frac{N_0}{N}\end{aligned}\]
\(N/N_0\)
the carbon-14 left, as a fraction of the level in living tissue
Watch out. The ratio inside the logarithm is \(N_0/N\), which is at least 1, so the age comes out positive. Writing \(N/N_0\) gives a negative age.
